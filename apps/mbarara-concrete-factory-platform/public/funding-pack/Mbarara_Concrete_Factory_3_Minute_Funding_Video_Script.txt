Mbarara Integrated Concrete Products Factory is a lean Phase One manufacturing project for Western Uganda. The funding ask is one hundred and thirty million Uganda shillings only. The model protects a two million shilling cash buffer and limits the first startup cost to one hundred and twenty eight million shillings. This is not a luxury launch. It is designed around essentials: a semi automatic block and paver machine, starter moulds, a curing yard, utilities, pallets, raw materials, basic laboratory tools, safety, launch labour and customer acquisition.

The founder is Hillary Arindamukama, a twenty five year old international student at Friedrich Alexander University Erlangen Nuremberg, studying Clean Energy Processes. The founder story is simple: use technical education, discipline and international exposure to build useful manufacturing capacity at home in Uganda. The project is meant to create jobs, improve construction quality, and serve contractors with predictable supply.

The factory will begin with products that can sell quickly and be controlled properly: six inch hollow blocks, eight inch hollow blocks, solid blocks, sixty millimetre pavers, eighty millimetre pavers and kerbstones. Ready mix concrete, culverts, heavy drainage products, additional moulds, dedicated trucks and advanced laboratory equipment are deferred until cash flow can support them.

The financial simulator uses Uganda shilling inputs only. Under the current estimated Phase One sales scenario, monthly revenue is one hundred forty million twenty thousand shillings. Monthly production cost is about ninety three million four hundred fifty six thousand shillings after updating cement and diesel assumptions. Monthly expenses are fourteen million one hundred thousand shillings. The model therefore estimates monthly net profit of about thirty two million four hundred sixty three thousand five hundred seventy eight shillings, which is above the twenty million shilling monthly profit target.

The first sales campaign should focus on Mbarara City, Rwampara, Isingiro, Ntungamo, Bushenyi, Ibanda, Kiruhura, Sheema, Lyantonde, Kabale and Rukungiri. Priority customers are contractors, hardware stores, property developers, schools, hospitals, fuel stations, NGOs, churches, hotels, government projects and residential builders.

The main risk is data confidence. Prices, machinery quotations, supplier terms and competitor information must be re verified before money is committed. The operating response is strict: verify quotations, require deposits, test batches, document curing, control rejections, avoid unpaid deliveries, and expand only after repeat demand is proven.

The funding request is clear: raise one hundred and thirty million Uganda shillings to launch Phase One, verify supplier quotations, secure the site, buy only essential equipment, produce the first products, and report monthly progress through the website, PDF report, financial simulator and quality control system.
